How do you find out about spontaneous things while they're still happening?

Whistly is the answer to that question.

Traditional social media is cluttered. A lot of the time it's hours later when someone posts a photo or a story, and only if you happen to be scrolling at the right time and the algorithm shows it to you.

I keep missing things I wished I had caught on time. So I'm building communities of thing finders, starting in Winnipeg, where we can find out faster about the things we're interested in, and help each other.

How Whistly works

  1. Subscribe to what you care about

    Pick the kinds of moments and the places you want to hear about: Aurora, Rainbow, Moon Halo, Birds in Backyard, Tornado, Hail, Wild Animals, Mushrooms, Nature Finds, Gifting, Harvest Share, Safety Events and many others. Anyone can subscribe to any area, big or small, near or far, anywhere in the world.

  2. Someone nearby spots it and posts a signal

    When you see something fleeting and unpredictable, you post a quick signal with a photo. You can only post from where you are physically standing, or very near your actual location, so the pins are real.

  3. You get the alert while it's happening

    Everyone subscribed to that kind of event in that area gets a targeted push notification, so people can catch it instantly, before it's gone.

Posts can be anonymous, so sharing a sighting never means sharing who you are.

You don't need friends or followers, and you don't wait to be approved into separate community groups.

Why I built Whistly

A few years ago, when I came to Canada, I dreamed of seeing the northern lights, like many others do. I was surprised to discover how many locals haven't seen them, or have seen them only a handful of times. I started following some Facebook pages and realized it wasn't that uncommon for the northern lights to happen here. Catching them on time was the problem.

Facebook is a cluttered, noisy place. I don't know how many times I've opened it, seen an amazing aurora photo, and thought: “Damn, I missed it again!” 😄

Then I downloaded aurora forecast apps to see if conditions might be good. But I kept going out in very cold weather only to find that it was too cloudy, or the moon was brighter than I wanted. It wasn't easy to find the right moment, and I ended up torn between the fear of missing out and the fear of losing my job the next day 😃

This story is probably similar to many others, but this is where mine starts. The northern lights inspired Whistly, an app that helps local communities of sky-watchers, wildlife photographers, nature lovers and all sorts of thing finders connect with each other.

Other apps are great for analyzing data and forecasting conditions. I wanted something different: a quick alert from someone nearby who is actually seeing it right now. Whistly fills the gap that exists when we rely on data alone.

It's not only for event chasers

A while back, we noticed a deer trapped in a tomato cage. We reached out to the city, but not knowing how fast they would respond, we also tried to share the word in local community groups. Even as a group member, getting a post approved takes so long that the information becomes irrelevant. After a conversation, an officer freed the deer, and we edited the post to say she was free – but even that was published a week late. That's not ideal when you're trying to share fleeting, spontaneous events.

Whistly is built for instant, real-time communication when something worth sharing is happening. It's one app for all thing finders around the globe. Nature photographers and event chasers are the community I'm building it for, and Winnipeg, where the original idea came from, is where it starts.

Where Whistly is today

It's very early, with very few signals so far. For now I'm looking for local community volunteers, starting in Winnipeg but wherever you are, to try the idea with me: to explore what works, what doesn't, and where it helps people most to catch things they would otherwise miss.

The app is free and has no ads, and I'm a solo developer.

If this sounds interesting for your community, download it, check it out, and tell me what you think.

You can send feedback, questions or any inquiry through Help & Support inside the app.
